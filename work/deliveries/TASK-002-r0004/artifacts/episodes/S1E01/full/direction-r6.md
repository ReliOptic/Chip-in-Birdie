The director rejected R5's interchangeable objects, viewpoint, backgrounds and

## Editorial contract

The opening follows a familiar company through an unfamiliar bottleneck:
NVIDIA had a successful graphics processor, yet manufacturing constrained
shipments. Rewind to Chang's 1985 question and the 1987 creation of TSMC, then
return to the stakes of that manufacturing relationship. The benefit is the
ability to finance a chip business separately from a fabrication business;
the cost is real production dependency and yield risk, not a vocabulary quiz.

Three considered treatments: a dark archive room; a warm museum catalogue;
and a high-contrast documentary edit. Chosen: the third. Full-bleed photographs,
period objects, bright paper documents, cobalt questions and red annotation.
Vox is a reference for evidence-to-explanation editing, not copied branding,
logo, script or footage. No fake historical images or re-enacted archival claims.

## Picture grammar

- English narration, Korean/English/off captions; 16:9, finite, no loop.
- At least 6 minutes of complete argument; actual voice samples govern timing.
- Photographs lead. Macro crops reveal actual markings. Product date and photo
  date remain distinct. Contemporary Taiwan locator imagery is not 1987 footage.
- The 1989 Erfurt factory image is labeled East Germany, never Taiwan or TSMC.
- Film geometry from earlier revisions remains in history but is not a repeated
  background in this cut. Illustrative diagrams are labeled, not historical data.
- Typography, brightness, composition and distance change with the argument.
  Hard cuts, match cuts and deliberate holds; no orbit, breathing loop or random
  camera drift. Annotation enters on evidence, not on every spoken word.
- Technical references: aimotion 24 (layered paper compositing / explicit masks),
  131 (minimum-jerk focused reveal / exposure hierarchy). Original subjects,
  palettes and sequence are not borrowed. Most shots are original photo edits.
- UI uses Unslop's hierarchy and quiet controls; those UI rules do not prohibit
  assertive documentary display typography or editorial hard cuts.
